Product Name: COMMON

Product Type: Activity-First Social Discovery Platform

Core Concept:

People often want to meet new people but find it difficult to initiate conversations or build connections with strangers. Traditional social platforms primarily focus on profiles, followers, likes, and online interactions.

COMMON takes a different approach.

Instead of asking users to connect with strangers first, COMMON encourages them to discover something they genuinely enjoy doing and meet people through that shared activity.

The platform follows this core journey:

Interest → Activity → People → Shared Experience → Repeated Interaction → Friendship

Examples of activities could include:

Going for a walk or morning run.

Playing badminton, football, or other sports.

Visiting a café.

Exploring a new place.

Photography walks.

Reading sessions.

Board games.

Cycling.

Hiking and outdoor activities.

Learning a new skill.

Attending workshops.

Music and creative activities.

Study sessions.

Other hobbies and shared interests.

The platform should make it easier for users to find activities, discover like-minded people, participate in shared experiences, and develop friendships naturally.

2. TARGET USERS

The platform should support people who:

Want to meet new people.

Have recently moved to a new city.

Want to find companions for their hobbies.

Want to participate in group activities.

Want to explore their interests with others.

Find it difficult to initiate friendships.

Want to become part of local communities.

Prefer meeting people through shared experiences rather than random social matching.

The platform should support both individual users looking for activities and users who want to organise activities for others.

This is one of the most important sections of COMMON.

Users should be able to discover activities they might want to participate in.

E. Activity Interaction

Users should be able to:

Open an activity.

View its details.

Join an activity.

Leave an activity.

Identify activities they have already joined.

View participant information where permitted.

Access the organiser's profile.

Prevent users from joining the same activity multiple times.

Respect the activity's participant capacity when a maximum capacity is defined.

Provide appropriate feedback when an activity is full, cancelled, unavailable, or no longer accepting participants.

Every activity should have an individual detail page.

Display:

Activity title.

Full description.

Category.

Date and time.

Location.

Organiser details.

Participant count.

Participant capacity, if defined.

Participant information.

Activity status.

Join or leave action.
Required functionality

Users can view all relevant information before joining.

Users can join an available activity.

Users can leave an activity they previously joined.

The participant count updates after successful joining or leaving.

The same user cannot join multiple times.

Users cannot join an activity that has reached its capacity.

Activity organisers should be identifiable.

Activity status should be reflected accurately.

Unauthenticated users should be directed to authentication before performing restricted actions.

The activity detail page should clearly communicate the experience a user is joining.

7. ACTIVITY CREATION AND MANAGEMENT

COMMON should support users who want to organise activities for other people.

Provide activity creation functionality.

The activity creation process should collect:

Activity title.

Description.

Category.

Date.

Start time.

Duration, if applicable.

Location.

Participant capacity, if applicable.

Relevant image or media, if supported.

Activity management

The activity organiser should be able to:

Create an activity.

View activities they have organised.

Edit their own activity.

Cancel or delete their own activity.

View participants.

Monitor participant count.

Only the activity organiser or an authorised administrator should be allowed to modify or cancel an activity.

Validate required fields and prevent invalid dates, times, or participant capacities.

Navigation to registration.

Maintain authenticated user sessions.

10. PEOPLE DISCOVERY

People discovery is an important part of COMMON, but it must follow the activity-first philosophy.

The objective is not simply to display random profiles.

The platform should help users discover people through shared interests and participation in similar activities.

Possible discovery information includes:

Name.

Profile picture.

Location, where appropriate.

Shared interests.

Activities they participate in.

Common categories or hobbies.

Users should be able to open another user's public profile and understand their interests and participation.

The discovery system should support finding people with common interests.

Do not turn this into a swipe-based dating or matchmaking application.

11. COMMUNITIES AND GROUPS

Support the concept of communities formed around shared interests and activities.

A community or circle can represent people who share a common interest or repeatedly participate in similar activities.

Examples:

Photography Community.

Weekend Cycling Group.

Book Lovers.

Football Community.

Hiking Group.

Board Game Community.

The functionality should support the relationship between interests, activities, and groups.

Where implemented, users should be able to:

Discover relevant communities.

View community information.

See related activities.

Join or leave a community.

View its members.

Access community-related interactions.

Community membership and activity participation should be treated as separate concepts.

12. MESSAGING AND COMMUNICATION

Include messaging as a platform capability for facilitating communication between connected users or activity participants.

The communication functionality should support:

Viewing conversations.

Opening a conversation.

Sending messages.

Viewing previous messages.

Displaying message timestamps.

Identifying the sender.

Maintaining conversation history.

Messaging should support communication related to shared activities and connections.

Do not allow unauthorised users to access private conversations.

If real-time messaging is not supported by the existing backend, establish a functional implementation appropriate to the available technology and clearly distinguish it from real-time functionality.

16. SEARCH, FILTERING AND DATA ACCURACY

Search and filtering should operate on actual application data.

Requirements:

Search should return relevant activities.

Category filtering should work correctly.

Location filtering should use available location data.

Date filtering should return appropriate activities.

Activity counts should remain accurate.

Joining and leaving should update participant relationships correctly.

User profiles should display their actual activities and interests.

Organisers should see their own created activities.

Avoid hardcoded results being presented as live user data.

If mock data is used during development, keep it clearly separated from the actual backend and make the application easy to connect to real data later.

18. VALIDATION, SECURITY AND ERROR HANDLING

Implement proper validation and error handling throughout the application.

Important requirements:

Validate forms.

Protect authenticated routes.

Enforce user ownership and permissions.

Prevent duplicate activity participation.

Prevent invalid activity creation.

Prevent unauthorised activity editing.

Protect private user information.

Protect private conversations.

Handle unavailable or deleted activities.

Handle empty search results.

Display useful error messages.

Provide loading and success feedback.

Handle backend failures without breaking the application.

Never rely only on frontend validation for security-sensitive operations.

19. RESPONSIVENESS AND ACCESSIBILITY

The application must work across:

Desktop computers.

Laptops.

Tablets.

Mobile devices.

All major functionality should remain accessible across different screen sizes.

Ensure:

Semantic page structure.

Accessible form labels.

Keyboard navigation.

Appropriate input validation messages.

Meaningful image alternative text.

Accessible interactive controls.

Proper handling of loading and error states.

Do not sacrifice functionality on smaller screens.

22. IMPORTANT PRODUCT PRINCIPLES

These principles must guide implementation:

COMMON is activity-first, not profile-first.

Shared experiences are the foundation of social discovery.

Users should be able to discover activities before being pushed to connect with strangers.

Activity participation should be a central platform interaction.

Profiles should communicate interests and participation.

Communities should form around shared interests and experiences.

The platform should encourage meaningful interactions rather than superficial engagement.

The application should not become a dating application.

The application should not become a generic social media feed.

The core experience should remain focused on helping people find common ground.
